This brief covers AI news from 2026-10-19 UTC.

Regulators and state attorneys general moved on OpenAI from three directions, and a federal appeals court backed Thomson Reuters in an AI training case.

## Eight states sue OpenAI over safety claims and kids' data

**What happened:** Eight US state attorneys general sued OpenAI on Wednesday, alleging it misled the public about product safety and misused children's data. The same day, the European Commission's AI Office sent requests for information on security, external evaluation and model monitoring.

**Why it matters:** Teams shipping on OpenAI's API now face disclosure and monitoring questions from EU regulators, so keep evaluation and usage records you can hand over.

[Source: TechPolicy.Press](<https://www.techpolicy.press/openai-faces-lawsuits-subpoenas-and-eu-scrutiny-in-a-single-week/>)

## FTC subpoenas OpenAI and Anthropic over consumer harms

**What happened:** Axios reported Thursday that the US Federal Trade Commission subpoenaed OpenAI and Anthropic over consumer harms, according to TechPolicy.Press. The subpoenas landed in the same week as the state suits and the EU information requests.

**Why it matters:** Both major model vendors are now answering federal demands for documents, a reminder that vendor terms and safety claims can change under scrutiny.

[Source: TechPolicy.Press](<https://www.techpolicy.press/openai-faces-lawsuits-subpoenas-and-eu-scrutiny-in-a-single-week/>)

## Appeals court upholds Thomson Reuters win in AI training suit

**What happened:** An appeals court upheld Thomson Reuters' win in its AI training lawsuit, The Verge reported. The ruling leaves the earlier trial outcome in place.

**Why it matters:** Training data provenance just got more expensive to ignore; builders scraping or licensing corpora should expect the same arguments to be tested again.

[Source: The Verge](<https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1013500/appeals-court-upholds-thomson-reuters-win-in-ai-training-lawsuit>)

## Apple benchmark grades coding agents by serving the engine

**What happened:** Apple AIML researchers published RSI-Inference Bench on October 16. Agents get a pinned vLLM or SGLang commit, a model and 1 to 8 H100 or B200 GPUs, work offline for up to 8 hours, and are graded by building and serving the edited engine across 37 tasks.

**Why it matters:** It measures whether an agent can land day-0 model support in real inference stacks, closer to the work infra teams actually assign.

[Source: MindPattern](<https://mindpattern.ai/s/2026-10-18-apple-s-rsi-inference-bench-asks-coding-agents-to-add-day-0-model-support-to-vllm-and>)

## Intel and Qualcomm reportedly circle Tenstorrent in $5B+ talks

**What happened:** Intel and Qualcomm are reportedly in talks to acquire AI chip startup Tenstorrent, founded by Jim Keller, in a deal potentially exceeding $5 billion, per AI Startups Daily.

**Why it matters:** Consolidation among accelerator vendors could reshape which hardware backends inference frameworks prioritize supporting.

[Source: AI Startups Daily](<https://aistartupsnews.com/categories/company-updates/>)

## Anthropic revenue hits $16.8B but misses IPO expectations

**What happened:** Anthropic reported $16.8 billion in third-quarter revenue, but growth slowed and the figure fell short of investor expectations ahead of a potential $2 trillion IPO, AI Startups Daily reported.

**Why it matters:** Pricing and capacity plans at a major model vendor now hinge on public-market scrutiny, so expect contract terms to be negotiated harder.

[Source: AI Startups Daily](<https://aistartupsnews.com/categories/company-updates/>)
