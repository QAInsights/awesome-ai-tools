This brief covers AI news from 2026-11-28 UTC.

A 111-page OpenAI preprint takes on a 1969 open problem, Sber drops a 708B open-weight model, and TSMC builds a kit for agentic chip design.

## OpenAI preprint claims matrix multiplication exponent is exactly 9/4

**What happened:** A 111-page preprint credited to OpenAI claims to prove that the exponent of square matrix multiplication is exactly 9/4 over every field. Bytechap, citing The New Stack, describes it as closing a theoretical gap open since 1969.

**Why it matters:** If the proof holds, the ceiling on fast matrix multiply is settled math, not a shipping speedup, so treat it as theory until someone implements it.

[Source: Core AI Innovations](<https://coreaiinnovations.com/>)

## Sber open-sources 708B GigaChat 4 under MIT licence

**What happened:** Sber published the GigaChat 4 family on Hugging Face and GitHub on 26 November, with the 708B flagship under MIT and a 37B sibling under a noncommercial licence. The release ships a 1M context window, FP8, BF16 and GGUF checkpoints, and scores 80.89 on SWE-bench Verified.

**Why it matters:** A permissively licensed 708B model with a 1M context gives teams a self-hostable option for long-context coding work without a vendor API.

[Source: AI Compass](<https://ashutoshai.com/ai-compass/2026/11/27/>)

## TSMC unveils AI Design Kit so agents can drive chip design flows

**What happened:** At its 2026 Europe OIP Ecosystem Workshop in Munich, TSMC showed its AI Design Kit, process-specific technical information and design methodologies that let AI agents automate physical implementation and optimization of chips. It complements the existing process design kits.

**Why it matters:** Agent tooling is moving into the foundry flow itself, which changes what design automation teams will be expected to orchestrate against TSMC processes.

[Source: Tom's Hardware](<https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/semiconductors/tsmc-gives-a-sneap-peak-at-ai-design-kit-agentic-ai-with-foundry-expertise>)

## Microsoft links China and North Korea groups to ARTEX agents

**What happened:** Microsoft Threat Intelligence says groups linked to China and North Korea, plus criminal gangs, use ARTEX, an agentic framework that runs standard hacking tools with little human input. One campaign logged more than 151, 000 failed sign-ins, peaking at 2, 900 a minute.

**Why it matters:** Agent-driven credential attacks raise the volume your auth layer has to absorb, so rate limits and lockout rules matter more than they did against manual attempts.

[Source: Core AI Innovations](<https://coreaiinnovations.com/>)

## OpenAI fixes billing bug, so GPT-6.2 limits arrive sooner

**What happened:** OpenAI says it had incorrectly discounted usage of GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol. Tibo Sottiaux said on X that users will now reach limits 40% faster on Astra and 25% faster on Sol than last week.

**Why it matters:** Budget your token spend against the corrected accounting, because the same workload now burns through plan allowances noticeably faster.

[Source: Information Vampire](<https://informationvampire.com/section/labs>)

## JERA in talks with Anthropic and Meta on data centers by power plants

**What happened:** Nikkei Asia reports Japan's JERA has entered talks with Anthropic and Meta to build AI data centers near its power plants. TradingView's account of the story also lists Microsoft among the companies in the talks.

**Why it matters:** Power siting is becoming the binding constraint on capacity, so where inference runs will increasingly follow generation, not the other way round.

[Source: Nikkei Asia](<https://exa.ai/library/news/story/j3ht0xl0tryx6pxd6slblgd6v>)

## ChronoScale raises $750M with $7.5B frontier lab deal attached

**What happened:** Applied Digital's SEC-filed investor presentation states its cloud spinoff ChronoScale raised $750 million in financing. It also describes a six-year development partnership with an unnamed frontier AI lab carrying up to $7.5 billion in total contract value.

**Why it matters:** More contracted compute capacity is being locked up years ahead, which shapes what cloud pricing and availability look like for everyone else.

[Source: ComputeLabs Research](<https://www.computelabs.ai/research/2026-11-27>)
