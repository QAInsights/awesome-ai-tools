This brief covers AI news from 2026-10-14 UTC.

Google's newest frontier model is live for developers, and the day brought agent identity, on-device reasoning, and a big routing acquisition.

## Google ships Gemini 4 Argon to developers and enterprises

**What happened:** Google made Gemini 4 Argon globally available via the Gemini API in AI Studio and the Gemini platform, in Google Antigravity for AI Ultra subscribers, and in the Gemini app and AI Mode in Search. It launches at an introductory $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens, rising to $4 and $20 after November 30, 2026.

**Why it matters:** Builders get a frontier model for long-horizon coding and enterprise workflows at a temporary discount, so cost planning should account for the November price change.

[Source: Google](<https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-4-argon/>)

## Workday signs deal to acquire agentic identity platform C1.ai

**What happened:** Workday announced a definitive agreement to acquire C1.ai, an agentic identity platform that connects to email, CRM, code repositories, finance systems, and cloud infrastructure to grant and revoke access for people and agents. Workday cited C1.ai research that 95% of large U.S. companies already run autonomous agents, while Deloitte found only 21% report a mature way to govern them.

**Why it matters:** Teams deploying agents into enterprise systems will increasingly need identity and access controls that treat agents like employees, which is where Workday is aiming.

[Source: Workday](<https://investor.workday.com/news-and-events/press-releases/news-details/2026/Workday-to-Acquire-C1-ai-a-Leading-Agentic-Identity-Platform-for-People-and-Agents/default.aspx>)

## Liquid AI releases LFM2.5-24B-A2B for on-device agents

**What happened:** Liquid AI published LFM2.5-24B-A2B, a 24B-parameter reasoning model with 2B active parameters per token, built to run agents locally. It scored 48.09 on LiveClaw versus 38.19 for Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, runs at 184 tok/s on an Apple M5 Max, and reaches up to 21K output tok/s on a single NVIDIA B300 GPU.

**Why it matters:** Developers can run tool-calling agents continuously on their own hardware instead of paying per token for every reasoning step in the cloud.

[Source: Liquid AI](<https://huggingface.co/blog/LiquidAI/lfm25-24b-a2b>)

## Physical Intelligence opens Pi API beta for robot models

**What happened:** Physical Intelligence launched a beta API that lets developers upload data, run fine-tuning on managed compute, and access fine-tuned robotic foundation models through a hosted inference gateway. The company said the latest model available through the API offers more data-efficient fine-tuning and improved capability for partners.

**Why it matters:** Robotics teams can adapt general-purpose robot models to new tasks and hardware without building their own ML infrastructure and data pipelines.

[Source: Physical Intelligence](<https://www.pi.website/blog/api-beta>)

## Stripe to acquire OpenRouter in a deal valued above $8B

**What happened:** Stripe agreed to acquire AI model-routing platform OpenRouter in a deal sources valued at more than eight billion dollars, according to a report citing Reuters. The same report said Google entered a custom-chip partnership with Marvell that includes up to twelve billion dollars in share purchases.

**Why it matters:** A payments company owning the layer that routes requests across model providers could change pricing, billing, and provider choice for teams that build on multi-model stacks.

[Source: CNNBC](<https://cnnbc.com/stripe-and-google-commit-billions-to-expand-artificial-intelligence-infrastructure>)

## IBM Research cuts chip design inference cost with open models

**What happened:** IBM Research said an agent factory using open frontier models and distributed inference optimized quantum error correction hardware with a 52-times speedup over the baseline in high-level synthesis estimates for about $26 in inference cost. The same search budget cost $299 with an all-proprietary configuration.

**Why it matters:** Chip and hardware teams can run large agent-driven design space searches on open models at a fraction of proprietary token costs.

[Source: IBM Research](<https://research.ibm.com/blog/open-agent-factories-and-distributed-inference-for-chip-design>)

## CoreWeave brings NVIDIA Vera Rubin NVL72 into production

**What happened:** CoreWeave announced availability of NVIDIA Vera Rubin NVL72 systems with Spectrum-X 102.4T Ethernet networking, with Cognition as the first customer running production workloads on them. Cognition benchmarked Vera Rubin against a GB200 NVL72 baseline on a software engineering workload sampled from FrontierCode and saw up to a 4.8x increase in early tests.

**Why it matters:** More production capacity for agentic coding workloads means teams running large-scale training and inference have another hardware option to benchmark against.

[Source: Future Tech Markets](<https://futuretechmarkets.com/markets/from-training-to-production-nvidia-and-coreweave-close-the-loop-on-agentic-ai-19/>)
