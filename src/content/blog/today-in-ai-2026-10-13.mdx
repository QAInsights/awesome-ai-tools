This brief covers AI news from 2026-10-13 UTC.

Meta enters the frontier race with two new models, Cognition ships a merge-or-reject coding benchmark, and two enterprise software vendors buy their way into agentic analytics.

## Meta ships Muse Wonder, its first frontier model

**What happened:** Meta Superintelligence Labs released Muse Wonder and Muse Wonder Full, its first frontier models. Wonder Full scores 56 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, tying Claude Sonnet 5.5; standard Wonder scores 52 and costs $4 per million input tokens and $20 per million output tokens. Only Wonder is served through the Meta Model API.

**Why it matters:** Builders get a new frontier option through the Meta Model API, but the strongest variant stays locked behind a Meta subscription, so teams needing top scores cannot buy it per token.

[Source: VentureBeat](<https://venturebeat.com/technology/metas-new-muse-wonder-and-muse-wonder-full-models-achieve-frontier-performance-but-only-the-weaker-one-is-available-via-api>)

## Cognition's FrontierCode asks if maintainers would merge the code

**What happened:** Cognition launched FrontierCode 1.1, a coding benchmark built with open source maintainers that grades whether an agent's change to a real repository is good enough to merge. Leading models still fail more than 40% of the time; Claude Opus 5.5 leads with a 52% average score and 58% pass rate.

**Why it matters:** It gives teams a merge-readiness signal for coding agents instead of a pass-the-tests number, which is closer to what actually blocks agent-written pull requests.

[Source: Artificial Analysis](<https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/frontiercode>)

## Salesforce signs deal to acquire analytics startup Loops

**What happened:** Salesforce announced a definitive agreement to acquire Loops, an AI analytics platform that monitors business performance and explains why metrics change. Salesforce plans to fold Loops' technology and staff into Tableau after the deal closes, expected in early November of its fiscal year 2027.

**Why it matters:** Agentic analytics is moving into the Tableau stack, so teams building on Salesforce data should expect metric monitoring and anomaly explanation to arrive as platform features rather than separate tools.

[Source: Salesforce](<https://www.salesforce.com/news/stories/salesforce-signs-definitive-agreement-to-acquire-loops/>)

## Freshworks to buy Torii for AI and software spend governance

**What happened:** Freshworks signed a definitive agreement to acquire Torii, extending Freshservice into software management and AI spend governance. Torii tracks AI consumption to the token, attributes it to people and teams, and forecasts spend so IT can set limits and charge it back.

**Why it matters:** Token-level cost attribution is becoming a purchasable IT control, which matters to anyone whose AI bill is currently a single unallocated line item.

[Source: Freshworks](<https://ir.freshworks.com/news/news-details/2026/Freshworks-to-Extend-Freshservice-into-AI-and-Software-Spend-Governance-with-Agreement-to-Acquire-Torii/default.aspx>)

## HCLTech and Anthropic form unit to build enterprise agents

**What happened:** HCLTech announced a global partnership with Anthropic and will set up a dedicated business unit with a centre of excellence for agentic AI solutions, using Claude, Cowork and Claude Code. HCLSoftware became an Anthropic ISV partner and will embed Claude in products including HCL BigFix and HCL AppScan.

**Why it matters:** Enterprises buying agent work through integrators will increasingly get Claude-based stacks, so vendors building adjacent tooling should plan for that integration surface.

[Source: The Indian Express](<https://indianexpress.com/article/technology/artificial-intelligence/hcltech-signs-pact-with-anthropic-to-set-up-business-unit-10918907/>)

## Microsoft details Maia 300, its next custom inference chip

**What happened:** Microsoft announced Maia 300, its third-generation AI inference platform, and said it plans to expand Azure Maia to multi-gigawatt deployments starting in 2027. Microsoft claims Maia 300 delivers 40% more tokens per megawatt and up to seven times the token throughput of Maia 200 at rack scale.

**Why it matters:** Cheaper Microsoft-hosted inference is the practical payoff for builders on Azure, though the throughput and efficiency figures are Microsoft's own projections.

[Source: Microsoft](<https://news.microsoft.com/source/emea/2026/10/microsoft-announces-maia-300-its-next-generation-custom-ai-chip/>)

## Z.ai ships GLM-Legal, an agentic model for legal work

**What happened:** Z.ai released GLM-Legal, built on GLM-5.3-Flash with legal-corpus mid-training and reinforcement-learning post-training. The company reports a 19.2% all-pass rate on Harvey's Legal Agent Benchmark, 12.5 points above the base model. There are no downloadable weights and API access opens by application.

**Why it matters:** Legal is the latest vertical to get a purpose-trained agent model, but with gated access and no weights, teams cannot self-host or benchmark it without applying.

[Source: Frontier Now](<https://frontiernow.dev/en/news/glm-legal/>)
