This brief covers AI news from 2026-09-18 UTC.

Infrastructure money kept flowing, Anthropic put a coordinator agent in charge of parallel coding threads, and OpenAI pointed GPT-6 at law firms.

## Crusoe raises $3.9B at a $30.9B valuation for AI data centers

**What happened:** Crusoe said Thursday it raised $3.9 billion in a Series F round co-led by Atreides Management, Mubadala Capital and Valor Equity Partners, pushing its post-money valuation to $30.9 billion. The money funds existing sites including a large Abilene, Texas data center used by OpenAI, plus modular Spark AI factories.

**Why it matters:** More leased and modular compute capacity means another place for teams to buy GPU time, and a hedge against single-region capacity crunches.

[Source: TechCrunch](<https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/17/crusoe-raises-3-9b-to-build-massive-data-centers-and-small-modular-ai-factories/>)

## Anthropic ships Claude Code Projects with a coordinator agent

**What happened:** Anthropic launched a redesigned Projects feature for Claude Code in beta for select Pro and Max subscribers. A coordinator agent splits a goal into parallel cloud sessions, each on its own branch and repo copy, and resolves overlapping code like a merge conflict. Anthropic warned parallel threads burn usage limits faster.

**Why it matters:** Developers can hand off multi-part engineering work without manually splitting tasks, but should watch plan ceilings since every thread is a full Claude Code session.

[Source: The Verge](<https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/997134/anthropic-claude-code-projects>)

## OpenAI releases Astra for Law, a GPT-6 build for legal work

**What happened:** OpenAI released Astra for Law, a configuration of GPT-6 Astra with a dedicated legal search index and legal-specific instructions, available soon to API customers including Harvey and Legora and initially to Am Law 200 firms through a Trusted Access program. OpenAI also shipped 26 vendor plugins and ChatGPT for Word.

**Why it matters:** Legal tech vendors get a prebuilt retrieval and instruction layer instead of assembling their own case-law search, though the reported benchmark gains are vendor-run on a private split.

[Source: LawSites](<https://www.lawnext.com/2026/09/openai-releases-astra-for-law-a-gpt-6-model-configured-for-legal-work.html>)

## xAI launches voice and agent APIs as Galaxy wraps

**What happened:** xAI shipped three enterprise products at its Galaxy event: a Voice Agent API priced at $0.08 per minute for speech-to-speech, a no-code Voice Agent Builder in beta since July 1, and an Agent Tools API for custom tool integration. The launch closed a 72-hour demo where staffers built a company with Grok Bot.

**Why it matters:** Cheap speech-to-speech pricing lowers the cost floor for voice agents, though builders take on integration with the rest of the xAI stack.

[Source: Forkast](<https://forkast.news/xai-ships-three-grokbot-enterprise-products-as-galaxy-day-3-demonstrates-full-business-automation/>)

## Figure's Helix 2.5 works in 30 homes it has never seen

**What happened:** Figure introduced Helix 2.5, a neural network pretrained on its Index dataset of human behavior, which performed three whole-body tasks across 30 Bay Area homes with no data collection, fine-tuning or adaptation in those environments. Figure said Index pretraining alone raised zero-shot success from 9% to 56%.

**Why it matters:** Zero-shot generalization is the gate for robots that ship once and work anywhere, rather than needing per-site data collection before deployment.

[Source: Figure](<https://www.figure.ai/news/helix-2-5-zero-shot-30-home-generalization>)

## Z.ai runs GLM-5.3-Flash inference on 100, 000 Chinese chips

**What happened:** Z.ai published a technical account of building production inference for GLM-5.3-Flash on a cluster of more than 100, 000 Chinese-made AI accelerators, saying much of the work was done by an Infra Agent powered by GLM-5.3. It reported throughput roughly tripled and per-token cost comparable to mainstream Nvidia GPUs.

**Why it matters:** A working large-scale serving stack on non-Nvidia silicon is a data point for anyone weighing accelerator supply and cost outside the CUDA ecosystem.

[Source: aifuturefront.com](<https://aifuturefront.com/z-ai-details-glm-5-3-flash-inference-build-on-100000-chinese-chips/>)

## Unsealed filings show Microsoft and OpenAI fears on news scraping

**What happened:** Filings unsealed Thursday in the New York Times copyright case quote Microsoft Director of Applied Science Brent Hecht calling news scraping for AI training the largest theft of labor in human history, and ChatGPT head Nick Turley writing that publishers face an existential threat from products trained on their content.

**Why it matters:** The case shapes what training data builders can use without a license, and the filings show how the defendants' own staff framed the risk.

[Source: Ars Technica](<https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/09/microsoft-exec-called-ai-scraping-the-largest-theft-of-labor-in-human-history/>)
