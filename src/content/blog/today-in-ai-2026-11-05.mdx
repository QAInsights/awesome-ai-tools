This brief covers AI news from 2026-11-05 UTC.

Two big open-weight releases landed, OpenAI went shopping for Git tooling, and a Stanford team found most published LLM findings no longer reproduce.

## Tencent launches Hy4 with vision and computer use

**What happened:** Tencent announced Hy4, its latest large model, adding visual understanding and computer-use capabilities that let it drive command-line and graphical interfaces. It is available via API now, with model weights and system cards promised in the coming days.

**Why it matters:** Builders get an API today for GUI and CLI automation, but anyone planning to self-host has to wait for the weights to actually appear.

[Source: Tencent](<https://www.tencent.com/tencent-launches-hy4-joining-the-ranks-of-the-worlds-leading-open-source-ai-models/>)

## Moonshot posts Kimi K3.1 Pro weights to Hugging Face

**What happened:** Moonshot AI uploaded the full weights of Kimi K3.1 Pro, a 2.8-trillion-parameter sparse mixture-of-experts model with a 1-million-token context window, under a permissive license with conditions reserved for massive commercial services.

**Why it matters:** A trillion-scale open model with a million-token context gives infrastructure teams something real to benchmark against, though the hardware bill is the first thing to check.

[Source: Neural Brew](<https://neuralbrew.ai/en/categories/models-labs/>)

## OpenAI to acquire Pierre Computer Company for Codex

**What happened:** OpenAI announced it will acquire Pierre Computer Company, whose open-source developer tools already power parts of Codex, to expand Codex's tools for building, understanding and reviewing code. The deal still needs regulatory approval.

**Why it matters:** Git workflows are where agents and humans collide, so expect Codex to get tighter control over branching, review and merge steps in your repo.

[Source: OpenAI](<https://frontieraiwatch.com/en/models-tools>)

## Stanford reran 50 famous LLM findings, 44% did not hold

**What happened:** Owen Queen and James Zou reproduced 50 well-known experiments across 20 recent models. Capability failures such as hallucinated citations faded quickly, while sycophancy and judge models favoring their own outputs persisted.

**Why it matters:** If you cite a benchmark from a year or two ago to justify a model choice, rerun it on the model you actually ship.

[Source: Neural Brew](<https://neuralbrew.ai/en/categories/models-labs/>)

## Together Compiler tunes open models overnight with agents

**What happened:** Together AI launched Together Compiler, which uses teams of AI agents to rewrite the GPU code behind the models it serves. Together says it runs 252x more experiments than existing agent-based systems and beats hand-written expert code by up to 2.6x.

**Why it matters:** New open models can get faster on Together's cloud without customers doing anything, which changes how quickly a fresh release becomes cheap to serve.

[Source: Together AI](<https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20261104879153/en/Together-AI-Launches-Together-Compiler-for-Continual-Optimization-of-Open-Source-AI-Models>)

## Nooks takes $500M commitment from General Catalyst fund

**What happened:** Nooks secured a $500 million commitment from General Catalyst's Customer Value Fund to finance its sales and marketing push, and launched its Revenue Agent Platform. The structure takes a share of returns the capital generates rather than a traditional equity stake.

**Why it matters:** It is a reminder that agent startups are being financed on revenue-share terms now, a structure worth understanding before you sign one.

[Source: Reuters](<https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/ai-sales-startup-nooks-secures-500-million-in-funding-for-expansion-4998523>)

## Google ships GKE Workspaces for notebooks on its clusters

**What happened:** Google introduced GKE Workspaces, a self-hosted AI/ML development platform for Google Kubernetes Engine built on the open source Kubeflow Workspaces project, with a web dashboard for picking an IDE and a machine size such as GPU or TPU.

**Why it matters:** Researchers get persistent notebooks on shared accelerators without touching YAML, while platform teams keep control of which images and hardware are allowed.

[Source: Google Open Source Blog](<https://opensource.googleblog.com/2026/11/gke-workspaces-notebooks-on-google-ai-infrastructure.html>)
